Lower to 60 Hz while the phone is hot
When Samsung's heat protection holds the GPU back hard, apps cannot keep up with a fast monitor anyway (about 50 fps at 5120 × 2160). Then the monitor goes to 60 Hz at the same resolution for smooth 60 fps, and back to your choice once the phone has cooled down for a few minutes. Every mode change while an experimental option is on must be confirmed within 30 seconds, or the previous mode comes back by itself. So a black monitor fixes itself.
Compression (DSC)
Display Stream Compression, built into the phone's display chip but switched off by Samsung. With it the cable is no longer the limit, the monitor's decoder is: on an LG 40U990A 5120 × 2160 runs at 100 and 110 Hz in 10-bit colour (tested). Costs about 0.1 W; a higher refresh rate itself costs about 17 mW per Hz. Modes too fast for the monitor's decoder are crossed out in the list (the LG's own 120 Hz would stay black).
Check new monitors automatically
A monitor that was not checked yet is checked once when it connects (one extra reconnect). If it supports DSC, compression is switched on; keep it within 45 s or it goes off again by itself.
Extra refresh rates (custom timings)
Adds faster versions of the monitor's own native mode, as far as the link (17.28 Gbit/s), the monitor's stated maximum and, with compression, its decoder allow. Examples on an LG 40U990A: 70, 75 and 80 Hz in 6-bit colour without compression; 105 and 110 Hz with compression. Remembered for this monitor and applied again at every connection (one extra reconnect, about 10 s). Other monitors are always read with their own data.
Technical details

Refresh rate and resolution at the top are the mode the display engine drives the cable with (SurfaceFlinger). Link timing, DP link, Link utilisation and Pixel clock come from the line the kernel writes when the monitor connects, so they change only at the next (re)connection. The monitor's name and EDID are read from the connector on every refresh.

Frames per second counts the frames SurfaceFlinger composed for the monitor since the previous refresh. It drops when nothing on screen changes; with motion (scrolling, video, testufo.com) it should match the refresh rate.

Link utilisation is pixel clock × bits per pixel against what the link carries after 8b/10b coding. The phone always trains the link at HBR2 with 4 lanes: 17.28 Gbit/s of pixel data. That is the real ceiling: 5120 × 2160 @ 60 Hz only fits at 8-bit colour; 5120 × 2160 @ 80 Hz fits at 6-bit (the kernel picks the deepest colour that fits). If the lanes drop to 2 (some hubs), half is left.

Phone power is the whole phone's draw: the battery fuel gauge's system current times the system voltage, read once a second while this page is visible, averaged here over the last 30 seconds. It is the same whether the phone runs on its battery or on the monitor's power. Measured on a Fold8 with an LG 40U990A and a still DeX screen: 60 Hz about 2.0 W, 60 Hz with DSC about 2.15 W, 80 Hz (6-bit) about 2.4 W, 110 Hz with DSC about 3.0 W; DSC itself costs about 0.1 W, each extra Hz about 17 mW. Moving content costs more.

This page only measures while it is open and visible: once a second, about 50 ms of work each time. Closed, it costs nothing. The background watcher reads one file every 3 seconds.
